STREET FIGHTER 2010 - v1.01 TRANSLATION NOTES

Scope
- v1.01 is a narrow maintenance release based on the stable V12 / RC9 v1.0 build.
- The Japanese in-game script remains the primary source.
- The original Japanese manual/transcription is used for canonical terminology and context.
- No broad prose rewrite was performed after the source audit.

Manual-confirmed terminology retained
- Parasite
- Armored Insect
- Galaxy Police
- Open Power
- Dimensional Door
- Flip Shield
- Dagobah remains the intended planet spelling; the game's planet names deliberately include Star Wars references such as Tatooine and Dagobah.

v1.01 corrections
1. Record 5
   v1.0: EQUIP IT, AND YOU'LL BE ONE STEP CLOSER TO PERFECTION...
   v1.01: EQUIP IT, AND YOUR STRENGTH WILL COME ONE STEP CLOSER TO PERFECTION...
   Reason: restores the explicit Japanese reference to Kevin's strength approaching perfection.

2. Record 10
   v1.0: THAT'S RIGHT. OF ALL THE PARASITES CREATED BY DR. JOSE, YOU ARE THE GREATEST.
   v1.01: THAT'S RIGHT. YOU ARE THE GREATEST PARASITE THAT I, DR. JOSE, HAVE CREATED.
   Reason: Jose is the speaker. The Japanese uses a grandiose self-reference equivalent to "this Dr. Jose"; the v1.0 third-person English obscured that fact.

3. Record 20
   v1.0: KEVIN! LET ME TEST IT ON YOUR BODY!
   v1.01: KEVIN! I'LL TEST IT ON YOUR BODY!
   Reason: restores the assertive force of Jose's Japanese declaration rather than making it sound like a request for permission.

Proper-name policy
- Kevin's surname is rendered STRIKER in project documentation, following Capcom's later official English Character Guide. The Japanese name is ケビン・ストレイカー. The surname does not occur in the in-game dialogue, so this is a documentation/canon-style choice rather than a ROM-script change.
- BALLANTINE-TYPE remains the best-supported reading of バランタイン型; the manual does not supply a Latin spelling.

Source references

Audit result
- All other release dialogue and the opening crawl were left unchanged.
- Record 14 was rechecked and retained: its v1.0 wording accurately reflects the Japanese.
